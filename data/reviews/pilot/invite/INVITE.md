## Email

**Subject:** Can you spare 45 minutes for Sanskrit? A one-week review round

Dear [name],

I learnt Samskritam personally and machine learning professionally. Amid
today's mad rush of large language models, I feel we urgently need to think
about building better models on the foundation Maharishi Pāṇini gave us:
models that follow the grammar rather than guess at it. That is the aim of
Project Pāṇini, an open research project that combines the Aṣṭādhyāyī's
rules with machine learning to read Sanskrit reliably.

Models are only as good as their data, and this effort needs a lot of help
from the community to create, validate and correct datasets. Our training
data comes from the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit, and some of its word splits
at sandhi junctions are wrong. Before building a larger review app, I'm
running a one-week pilot with a small group of readers I trust, and I would
be grateful if you would take part.

**What you do.** You check 60 short items. Each shows a sentence with one
fused form highlighted and a proposed split, such as चापि = च + अपि. You
answer whether the split is right in that sentence; if it is wrong, you
type the correct words, in Devanagari or IAST. "Unsure" with a short note
is a perfectly good answer and never costs points. It takes about 30 to 45
minutes, on a phone or laptop.

**How to hand in.** When you finish, the worksheet seals your answers so
that no one can read them before the round closes. You then paste them into
a short form, which needs a free GitHub account. A bot confirms receipt
within a few minutes.

**Deadline.** Tuesday 1 December 2026, 23:59 UTC (Wednesday 2 December,
05:29 IST). Answers handed in later are not scored.

**Credit.** Results and a leaderboard are published automatically when the
round closes. If you tick the box in the worksheet, you are credited by name
there, in the dataset release and in the paper's acknowledgements;
otherwise you appear anonymously.

Start here: https://raghav-vk.github.io/panini-neuro-symbolic-ai/ (or scan
the attached QR code). The reviewer guide is linked from that page.

One request: please work on your own and don't discuss the items with
others until the round closes. Several people answer the same items, and
the point is to see where readers agree independently.

With thanks and regards,
Raghav Karnam
